# Rules, fair play and conduct

Sources: `www.sotonship.space/code-of-conduct`, `www.sotonship.space/prizes`, `www.sotonship.space/faq`, `www.sotonship.space/privacy`, and the "Track rules" section of each live brief. Fetched 16 October 2026.

## Eligibility

- Current university students only, **18 or over on 16 October 2026**, with **student ID checked at registration**. The desk only admits people on the confirmed list.
- You do not have to be a Southampton student and you do not need hackathon experience.
- This also satisfies the Hackathons UK rule that at least 80% of attendees are in formal education or have been within the last 12 months.
- Organisers, volunteers, mentors and judges cannot compete or win prizes.

## Teams

- Teams of two to five, all registered and physically present.
- **No solo entries in the judging.** Come alone and the team-forming board on Friday night plus the 18:00 icebreakers are how a crew gets formed.
- **Contradiction to confirm:** the judging page says "every team must have exactly 2 members", while every other page says teams of two to five and the track briefs say "teams of four (teams of two or three are fine too, and a solo entry needs an organiser's approval)". Ask a committee member.

## The event-wide rules

- **No project work before hacking opens at 20:00 on Friday.** Ideas are fine, code is not.
- Submissions close at **13:00 on Sunday**. The prize terms add that late submissions can still demo but are not judged.
- AI code-generation tools are allowed and encouraged, but the README must say what was generated and what was written by hand.
- The team owns everything it builds. Partners get a demo and nothing more.
- The pitch is a live demo of the working thing, not a slide deck.
- Each team enters **one track only**. If an idea fits two tracks, pick the one with the stronger user and business case.

## Fair play, and what causes disqualification

- Open-source libraries, frameworks, templates and pre-trained models are allowed. **Your own earlier projects are not** — a thin reskin of an existing AI tool will not be judged as your work, and publishing pre-written code as open source specifically to use it at the event is against the spirit of the rules.
- Do not scrape personal data, do not use real customer data, do not upload partner data to a public repository, and **do not connect an agent to a real email, calendar or bank account** — use the sandbox accounts provided.
- Never present cached data as live, or synthetic data as real.
- Plagiarism, misrepresenting what was built or by whom, breaking the conduct code, or **submitting the same project to another hackathon** all mean disqualification.
- Keep API keys and passwords out of the repository.

## Track-specific rules

Common to all three tracks:

- Build during the event and create the repository after hacking opens. State in the README which parts were AI-generated, what existed before the event, and who helped.
- Show where each number came from and when it was collected.
- Keep test cases separate from examples used to tune the system, and report sample sizes as well as results.
- Do not collect personal data you do not need, and do not send confidential or non-public information to an external AI service.

**Southampton City extra:** if the product covers safety, health or emergencies, link to official guidance rather than writing your own instructions.

**FinTech extra:** no real customer data, no connecting to a real bank account, card or wallet, and never move real money. Build tools that detect scams and fraud, not tools that create deepfakes, scam scripts or fake documents. If the product touches investment, credit or borrowing, link to official guidance such as the FCA or MoneyHelper instead of giving advice.

**Responsible AI extra:** credit research, code, models and data with dates and licences; label cached and synthetic data; avoid safety, fairness or compliance claims beyond what the tests support; do not use private customer records or sensitive personal data in the demo; test only systems you own or are authorised to test; use a sandbox with no real consequential decisions about people.

## Code of conduct

SotonShip runs under the **Hackathons UK Code of Conduct** in full, plus the event's own articles where those are stricter. It covers every participant, mentor, judge, volunteer, partner and organiser, for the whole weekend, in every space including online channels. It is agreed at registration on Luma and again at the door.

The code in short: no harassment of any kind in person or online (including offensive comments, unwelcome attention, intimidation, photographing people without consent); no alcohol unless served at an official moment with the venue's permission; no drugs; anyone who appears intoxicated will be asked to leave; respect the venue, which is a working space.

### Reporting something

You do not need to be certain and you do not need proof. Reports are heard in private, written down, and you are told what happens next. The conduct contact and the event lead decide together within the hour: a conversation, a warning, removal from the event, or contacting the university or the police. Your name is not shared with the person reported without your agreement. All routes can be used anonymously.

1. Speak to any SotonShip organiser or volunteer at the event.
2. Speak to a member of Hackathons UK staff at the event.
3. Call the Hackathons UK Incidents team: +44 300 131 3331.
4. Email incidents@hackathons.org.uk.
5. Email the organising team: aisoc@soton.ac.uk.

### Welfare

- The quiet room is open all weekend, no questions asked.
- Nobody has to stay. If you leave, tell the registration desk so they know you are safe.
- If you or a teammate is struggling, tell a volunteer or the welfare volunteer in the quiet room. The University of Southampton student wellbeing service is listed in the confirmation email.

## Privacy, as it affects the weekend

- Registration ran on Luma; the data controller is the University of Southampton AI Society.
- At the event: a signature on the registration list, and photographs unless opted out. Photography is opt-out at registration or at the desk, and a badge marker is issued.
- Organisers receive the attendee information needed to run and insure the event as a Hackathons UK partner event.
- Partners see the project at the demo fair, **not** registration details, unless the participant ticked the box to be put in touch.
- Winners' names and universities are published on social channels within an hour of the awards.
- Registration data held by the organisers is deleted within 30 days of the event.
- The `www` site itself collects nothing: no accounts, no forms, no cookies, no analytics. `live.sotonship.space` does have accounts, because it runs submissions and voting.
